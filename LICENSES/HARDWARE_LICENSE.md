# Synthetic Human Brain — Hardware License

Copyright 2026 ArkansasIo contributors.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the intended licensing boundary for original hardware designs developed as part of the Synthetic Human Brain project, including future hardware derived from the project's computational architecture.

## 2. Covered material

Subject to the exceptions below, original project hardware may include:

- electronic schematics;
- PCB layouts and board design files;
- firmware and embedded source code;
- FPGA/ASIC design source and hardware-description-language source;
- mechanical CAD and enclosure designs;
- interface specifications;
- bills of materials and assembly documentation;
- test fixtures and laboratory instrumentation designs; and
- hardware documentation and reference implementations.

## 3. Recommended hardware license

Original hardware design files are intended to use the CERN Open Hardware Licence Version 2 — Permissive (CERN-OHL-P-2.0), unless a specific hardware release states a different license.

The corresponding license text should be included with hardware releases. Software embedded in or distributed with hardware remains governed by the software license applicable to that software.

## 4. Third-party hardware

This license does not grant rights to third-party hardware or proprietary platforms. Hardware inspired by, connected to, or interoperating with an external platform is not thereby owned by this project.

In particular, references to Cortical Labs CL1 are integration and research references only. Nothing in this project grants a license to CL1 hardware, proprietary designs, trademarks, firmware, SDKs, biological material, or proprietary data.

## 5. Future hardware

If the project later produces a whole-brain research platform, neural-interface electronics, accelerator hardware, sensor systems, or laboratory computing hardware, each hardware release should identify its exact license and distinguish original project designs from third-party components.

## 6. Safety and research status

Project hardware specifications are research and engineering designs. They do not establish that a design is suitable for clinical use, implantation, diagnosis, treatment, or human experimentation. Any hardware intended for biological research must be developed and used under applicable institutional, regulatory, safety, and ethics requirements.
